This appendix is background. It gives the information geometry used in the book. The Fisher information is a Riemannian metric on a parametric family, unique up to scale among metrics invariant under sufficient statistics (Chentsov's theorem). Amari's alpha connections include the exponential and mixture connections, which are dual with respect to the Fisher metric; an exponential family is flat in natural coordinates and the mean coordinates are the dual flat coordinates, with the Legendre duality as the change between them. Kullback Leibler divergence is the canonical divergence of this dually flat structure and equals a Bregman divergence of the log partition function. The Pythagorean theorem for the I projection (the e geodesic projection onto an m flat submanifold) is stated and linked to moment conditioning, the I projection in Sanov's theorem, and alternating projections in maximum entropy algorithms in the maximum entropy algorithms.

## From the literature

Chentsov's theorem on the invariance of the Fisher metric (Chentsov 1982). Amari's alpha connections and dual flatness (Amari and Nagaoka 2000). The Pythagorean theorem for I projections (Csiszar 1975).
