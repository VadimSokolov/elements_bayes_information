This appendix is background. It is a toolkit of large deviation theory: the definition of a large deviation principle with a good rate function, Cramer's theorem for i.i.d. sample means, the Gartner Ellis theorem for dependent sequences through the limiting scaled cumulant generating function, Sanov's theorem for the empirical measure with rate given by Kullback Leibler divergence, and the contraction principle that pushes rates forward by minimization. It then states Varadhan's lemma and Laplace's method, the expansion behind the BIC penalty and the Bayes factor threshold, and the moderate deviation principle for the sum divided by root n times a_n, with a_n tending to infinity more slowly than root n, at speed a_n squared, where the rate x squared over two sigma squared is Gaussian and independent of higher cumulants for i.i.d. summands with variance sigma squared and a finite moment generating function near zero. The Gaussian rate explains why the moderate deviation thresholds of the testing chapters carry a universal leading term with model dependent constants. The statements are given without proof and with the hypotheses the text lists.
