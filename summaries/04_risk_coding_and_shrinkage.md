# Chapter 4. Risk, Coding, and Shrinkage

This chapter asks what a Bayesian decision costs, what the shortest description of data is, and how a sparse high dimensional signal should be shrunk, and answers on the moderate deviation scale, square root of log n. It treats Bayes risk and risk optimal testing thresholds for genome wide screening; nonparametric predictive Bayes risk, which splits the Kullback Leibler risk of a posterior predictive density into a Sanov term (the reverse I projection of the truth on the prior support) and a posterior contraction term, with Kolmogorov Smirnov thresholds and adaptive rates for Dirichlet process mixtures, Polya trees and logistic Gaussian processes; minimum description length, Bayesian evidence and singular BIC; Kolmogorov complexity; James Stein and global local shrinkage; and the horseshoe prior, its log pole, super efficiency and asymptotic Bayes optimality under sparsity (ABOS). The three nonparametric rates are conditional on a stated assumption, and the optimal Kolmogorov Smirnov truncation level, a moderate deviation threshold for non regular Ibragimov and Has'minskii models, and ABOS of the horseshoe and the beta horseshoe under exponential power noise are conjectures.

## From the literature

Rubin and Sethuraman (1965a, 1965b) on moderate deviations and Bayes risk efficiency; Aitchison (1975) and Clarke and Barron (1990) on predictive risk; Csiszar (1975) on I projection; Kleijn and van der Vaart (2006, 2012) on misspecified posteriors; Smirnov (1944) on the Kolmogorov Smirnov tail; Lavine (1992), Escobar and West (1995), Castillo (2017) and van der Vaart and van Zanten (2009) on nonparametric priors; Rissanen (1978) and MacKay (1992) on description length and evidence; Kolmogorov (1965), Chaitin (1966) and Solomonoff (1964) on algorithmic complexity; Stein (1956) and James and Stein (1961) on shrinkage; Carvalho, Polson and Scott (2010) and Polson and Scott (2010) on the horseshoe; Bogdan, Chakrabarti, Frommlet and Ghosh (2011) and Datta and Ghosh (2013) on ABOS; Ibragimov and Has'minskii (1981) on non regular models.

## New results

The chapter proves the predictive Bayes risk decomposition and the moderate deviation risk balancing thresholds; the tilted de Finetti limit (Polson and Zantedeschi 2025a) and the normal mean testing boundary (Datta, Polson, Sokolov and Zantedeschi 2026) are quoted without proof.

<!-- results:begin -->
- [Decomposition of nonparametric predictive Bayes risk into Sanov and contraction terms](../RESULTS.md#predictive_bayes_risk_decomposition) (Theorem 4.1, page 59; proved): The predictive KL Bayes risk splits into a population Sanov term, a posterior contraction term and a small cross term, so misspecified models have risk equal to the Sanov term and well specified ones have risk equal to the contraction term. First stated in Polson and Zantedeschi (2026i), Nonparametric Predictive Bayes Risk, working paper.
- [Conditional Sanov and tilted de Finetti](../RESULTS.md#conditional_sanov_tilted_de_finetti) (Theorem 4.5, page 61; proved): Conditioned on the empirical measure lying in a closed convex set, the predictive law of a fixed block converges to the I projection of the de Finetti baseline onto that set. First stated in Polson and Zantedeschi (2025a), De Finetti + Sanov = Bayes: Forward and Inverse Sanov in Bayesian Prediction, Statistical Science (revised short communication).
- [Bayes risk optimal Kolmogorov Smirnov threshold at the moderate deviation scale](../RESULTS.md#rubin_sethuraman_risk_balancing_ks) (Theorem 4.9, page 65; proved): The Bayes risk optimal Kolmogorov Smirnov threshold sits at about half the detection distance, with the exact constant 3/8 and risk of order n to the minus 25/16 at the boundary Delta_n = sqrt(2 log n) with prior odds n. First stated in Polson, Sokolov and Zantedeschi (2026b), Bayes Risk for Goodness of Fit Tests.
- [General moderate deviation risk balancing threshold for tests](../RESULTS.md#rubin_sethuraman_risk_balancing_general) (Theorem 4.11, page 68; proved): For any statistic with Gaussian type moderate deviation tails under null and alternative, the Bayes risk optimal threshold is half the detection scale plus a prior odds correction, and it is at least the multiple comparison scale sqrt(2 log b_n). First stated in Datta, Polson, Sokolov and Zantedeschi (2026), A New Look at Bayesian Testing.
- [Optimal truncation at the moderate deviation scale](../RESULTS.md#optimal_truncation_moderate_deviation_scale) (Conjecture 4.15, page 72; open): The book conjectures that the Bayes risk optimal truncation level for a Kolmogorov Smirnov truncated predictive density equals the detection distance, which is sqrt(2 log n) at the detection boundary, and gives a heuristic only. New in this book.
- [Master decomposition and the contraction term rate](../RESULTS.md#master_decomposition_contraction_rate) (Theorem 4.16, page 74; proved): Hellinger posterior contraction at rate epsilon_n gives a contraction term of order epsilon_n squared in the predictive risk decomposition, via the Wong and Shen conversion of Hellinger to KL. First stated in Polson and Zantedeschi (2026i), Nonparametric Predictive Bayes Risk, working paper.
- [Adaptive Dirichlet process mixture predictive rate](../RESULTS.md#adaptive_dpm_rate) (Theorem 4.18, page 75; conditional on the data driven bandwidth stays within constant factors of the optimal bandwidth, which the book assumes and does not prove): A Dirichlet process mixture of Gaussians attains the near minimax predictive KL rate for Holder smooth densities with a log n factor, given a sketch proof that assumes the bandwidth stays near its optimum. First stated in Polson and Zantedeschi (2026i), Nonparametric Predictive Bayes Risk, working paper.
- [Adaptive Polya tree predictive rate](../RESULTS.md#adaptive_polya_tree_rate) (Theorem 4.19, page 76; conditional on the marginal likelihood truncation level stays within O(1) of the optimal level, which the book assumes and does not prove): A canonical Polya tree attains the predictive KL rate n to the minus 2 beta over 2 beta plus 1 up to a log n factor for smoothness below one, given a sketch proof that assumes the selected level is near optimal. First stated in Polson and Zantedeschi (2026i), Nonparametric Predictive Bayes Risk, working paper.
- [Adaptive logistic Gaussian process predictive rate](../RESULTS.md#adaptive_logistic_gaussian_process_rate) (Theorem 4.20, page 77; conditional on the posterior contracts in Hellinger at the rate that van der Vaart and van Zanten (2009) prove for the squared exponential kernel; the remaining hypotheses of the master decomposition theorem (its density and Wong and Shen assumptions and its tail condition, that the posterior expected divergence D(pi^* || p) over laws P with d_H(P, Pi^* P_0) > M epsilon_n or pi^* > M_WS p is O(epsilon_n^2)), which the book assumes and does not check for this prior): A logistic Gaussian process prior attains the predictive KL rate with a polylog factor of exponent (4 beta + 1)/(2 beta + 1), assuming the posterior contraction rate of van der Vaart and van Zanten. First stated in Polson and Zantedeschi (2026i), Nonparametric Predictive Bayes Risk, working paper.
- [Sharp log log n term in horseshoe super efficiency](../RESULTS.md#horseshoe_super_efficiency_sharp_constant) (Theorem 4.27, page 122; conditional on the sharp lower bound, which the book gives only in outline, namely that n R_n = (1/2) log(n/2 pi) - 1/2 - E_0 log m_n(ybar) with the marginal density m_n of the sample mean kept below a constant multiple of log n, so that the sharp coefficient of log log n is proved only as an upper bound): At a zero mean the horseshoe's Cesaro average predictive Kullback Leibler risk is (half log n minus one log log n) over n, which sharpens the order of magnitude rate of Carvalho, Polson and Scott (2010) to a log log n coefficient, with the upper bound proved and the matching lower bound given only in outline. New in this book.
- [Pole of a scale mixture prior at the origin from its Levy measure](../RESULTS.md#scale_mixture_prior_pole_levy_measure) (Proposition 4.31, page 123; proved): A power law Levy measure of exponent gamma near zero gives a bounded prior density at the origin for gamma below one half, a logarithmic pole at one half, and a power pole of exponent 2 gamma minus 1 above one half. First stated in Polson, Sokolov and Zantedeschi (2026c), Horseshoe Priors and MDP.
- [Moderate deviation risk optimal testing boundary for the normal mean](../RESULTS.md#moderate_deviation_threshold_gaussian_testing) (Theorem 4.32, page 125; proved): For a point null test of a normal mean under zero one loss, the risk optimal boundary on the standardized mean squares to log n plus a prior and Fisher information dependent constant. First stated in Datta, Polson, Sokolov and Zantedeschi (2026), A New Look at Bayesian Testing.
- [Moderate deviation principle for non regular Ibragimov and Has'minskii models](../RESULTS.md#mdp_principle_for_ih_models) (Conjecture 4.41, page 138; open): For non regular models of the Ibragimov and Has'minskii classification whose limiting Bayes factor has a power law tail, the book conjectures that the optimal threshold is sqrt(gamma_p log n) in rate scaled coordinates. New in this book.
- [Modified asymptotic Bayes optimality under sparsity for the mild cusp](../RESULTS.md#modified_abos_for_mild_cusp) (Conjecture 4.43, page 139; open): For exponential power noise with shape between one half and one, the horseshoe is conjectured to be within a factor 2 over beta of the oracle Bayes risk. New in this book.
- [Weak ABOS for the horseshoe at the boundary beta one half](../RESULTS.md#weak_abos_at_the_boundary) (Conjecture 4.44, page 139; open): At exponential power shape one half the horseshoe is conjectured to be within a constant factor of the oracle but not asymptotically Bayes optimal. New in this book.
- [Failure of asymptotic Bayes optimality for the strong cusp](../RESULTS.md#abos_failure_for_strong_cusp) (Conjecture 4.45, page 140; open): For exponential power shape below one half the horseshoe Bayes risk is conjectured to be infinitely larger than the oracle risk in the limit. New in this book.
- [ABOS of the beta horseshoe for exponential power noise](../RESULTS.md#abos_of_the_beta_horseshoe) (Conjecture 4.48, page 142; open): A beta horseshoe prior matched to the exponential power shape is conjectured to be asymptotically Bayes optimal under sparsity, for shapes in (1/2, 2) with one tuning and for shapes at most 1/2 with another. New in this book.
