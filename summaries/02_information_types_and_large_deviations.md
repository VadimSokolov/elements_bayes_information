# Chapter 2. Information, Types, and Large Deviations

This chapter builds the information theoretic engine: the method of types, which bounds the probability of a type by exp of minus N D(Q||P) up to a polynomial factor; entropy, relative entropy (Kullback Leibler divergence) and mutual information; Stirling bounds and the equipartition property; and the Heath and Sudderth proof of de Finetti's theorem as the method of types on a scalar lattice. It proves Sanov's theorem for a positive law on a finite alphabet and a closed convex set of laws equal to the closure of its interior, through the dominant type, with the I projection and the divergence gap, and states the conditional limit theorem (Gibbs conditioning principle) of van Campenhout and Cover and Csiszar. It covers exponential families and maximum entropy, Cramer's theorem and the Legendre transform, Stein's lemma and Chernoff information, the three deviation scales, equivalence of ensembles, and the case for relative entropy among divergences.

## From the literature

- The method of types, in its standard form for finite alphabets.
- De Finetti's theorem for binary sequences, by the Heath and Sudderth (1976) argument on a type lattice.
- Sanov's theorem (Sanov 1957) and the I projection (Csiszar 1975, 1984).
- The conditional limit theorem and Gibbs conditioning (van Campenhout and Cover 1981; Csiszar 1984).
- Cramer's theorem, Stein's lemma and Chernoff information, and the maximum entropy principle (Jaynes 1957).

## New results

This chapter fixes notation and presents known results; its recasting of Heath and Sudderth as the method of types on a scalar lattice is exposition of a classical theorem.
