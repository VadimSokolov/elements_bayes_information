A test is a decision taken on evidence, and this chapter separates three questions that are often run together: what the evidence is, why acting on it controls error, and where to draw the line. The evidence is the likelihood ratio, canonical under logarithmic loss, an e variable and e process whose validity comes from the supermartingale property and Ville's inequality at every stopping time. The line is drawn by a Bayes risk calculation on the moderate deviation scale: the Bayes boundary for a point null rejects when the squared standardized statistic exceeds (dimension plus prior order) times log n, between the central limit scale of fixed critical values and the large deviation scale of fixed alternatives. The chapter states the moderate deviation principle and derives the fixed sample threshold and the sequential stopping time, with Gaussian fluctuations on the square root of log b scale. It gives the risk optimal Bayes factor threshold and the Rubin and Sethuraman analysis of the boundary, the order of the minimal Bayes risk, and Bayes risk efficiency compared with Pitman and Bahadur efficiency.

## New results

The new results are the canonical sequential evidence theorem with its liftability, normalized maximum likelihood tradeoff and log loss companions, and the risk optimal Bayes factor threshold.

<!-- results:begin -->
- [Likelihood ratio is the canonical sequential evidence under log loss](../RESULTS.md#canonical_sequential_evidence) (Theorem 6.1, page 195; proved): The Bayes test rejects when the prior predictive likelihood ratio exceeds a cost determined threshold, and that ratio is an e variable and the unique maximizer of expected log growth among e variables. First stated in Polson, Sokolov and Zantedeschi (2026a), Bayes, E-values, and Testing.
- [Sequential liftability of a codelength to an e process](../RESULTS.md#sequential_liftability_codelength) (Theorem 6.3, page 197; proved): A codelength gives an e process exactly when its one step factors form a sub probability kernel, which fails for normalized maximum likelihood codes at changing horizons. First stated in Polson, Sokolov and Zantedeschi (2026a), Bayes, E-values, and Testing.
- [Complexity validity tradeoff between minimax regret codes and e processes](../RESULTS.md#complexity_validity_tradeoff) (Corollary 6.4, page 198; proved): No code that attains the minimax individual sequence regret at every horizon gives an e process unless the normalized maximum likelihood laws of successive horizons are consistent. First stated in Polson, Sokolov and Zantedeschi (2026a), Bayes, E-values, and Testing.
- [Log loss is the unique scoring rule giving a martingale evidence process](../RESULTS.md#log_loss_uniqueness_evidence) (Proposition 6.5, page 198; proved): Among strictly proper scoring rules only the log score yields an evidence process that is a null martingale for every alternative, and other rules grow no faster than the log loss rate. First stated in Polson, Sokolov and Zantedeschi (2026a), Bayes, E-values, and Testing.
- [Risk optimal Bayes factor threshold on the moderate deviation band](../RESULTS.md#risk_optimal_bayes_factor_threshold) (Theorem 6.9, page 210; proved): For a normal mean and a point null, the Bayes factor and the risk optimal rejection cutoff, whose leading term log n is Jeffreys' threshold, hold with a uniform remainder on the moderate deviation band, with constants set by the prior density at the null, the variance and the prior odds. First stated in Datta, Polson, Sokolov and Zantedeschi (2026), A New Look at Bayesian Testing.
