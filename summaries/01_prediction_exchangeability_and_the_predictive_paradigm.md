This chapter asks what the primitive object of statistical inference is and answers that it is the predictive distribution of the next observation, with the parameter and prior as derived devices. It develops exchangeability and de Finetti's representation theorem, with a complete proof for binary sequences by the urn argument of Heath and Sudderth, whose coupling error for a block of m variables is m(m minus 1)/(2N), and the Hewitt and Savage extension through the directing measure. It previews Sanov's theorem with its I projection and the inverse Sanov theorem of Ganesh and O'Connell for posterior concentration. It then reads modern machine learning as Bayesian prediction: conformal prediction, in context learning of large language models, diffusion and score based generators, and amortized simulation based inference, each a device that manufactures a predictive rule, with martingale posteriors, calibration and coverage, and a translation table.

## From the literature

- De Finetti's theorem for exchangeable sequences (de Finetti 1937), with the proof for binary sequences by Heath and Sudderth (1976).
- The Hewitt and Savage extension to general spaces (Hewitt and Savage 1955).
- Sanov's theorem (Sanov 1957), stated here as a preview.
- The inverse Sanov theorem (Ganesh and O'Connell 1999, 2000), stated here as a preview.
- Martingale posteriors (Fong, Holmes and Walker 2023), conformal prediction (Vovk, Gammerman and Shafer 2005) and the Bayesian bootstrap (Rubin 1981), presented as predictive devices.
