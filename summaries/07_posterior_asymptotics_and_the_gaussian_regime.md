Why is the posterior so often approximately Gaussian, and when is it not? The chapter studies posterior asymptotics: Bernstein and von Mises theorems, posterior consistency and contraction rates, merging of opinions, martingale structure of marginal likelihoods and Bayes factors, and the Gaussian regime read off the curvature of the Kullback Leibler rate function. It treats the posterior as obeying a large deviation principle (a Bayesian Sanov theorem, reverse information projection) whose Hessian at the truth is the Fisher information, and adds the moderate deviation scale between the central limit and large deviation scales, with non coverage exponents of credible sets. It covers misspecified models (Kullback Leibler projection, sandwich covariance, Bartlett identity, Gibbs and tempered posteriors, learning rate calibration), mean field variational anti conservatism, dependent data with long run variance, proportional asymptotics for high dimensional regression, and failures of Bernstein and von Mises (mixed normality, non regular models, mixtures). It closes with a critique of martingale posteriors and the classes of Blackwell admissible, anytime valid and conformal rules.

## From the literature

Blackwell and Dubins merging of opinions (1962); Doob consistency (Doob 1949); the Bernstein and von Mises theorem; Berk's misspecified posterior concentration (1966); the Ghosal, Ghosh and van der Vaart contraction rate theorem (2000); the Kleijn and van der Vaart misspecified Bernstein and von Mises theorem (2012); posterior mixed normality after Heyde and Johnstone (1979); moderate deviations for the posterior (Eichelsbacher and Ganesh 2002); the conditional limit theorem (Csiszar 1984; Cover and Thomas 2006); Bissiri, Holmes and Walker Gibbs posteriors (2016); the complete class theorem of Wald and Le Cam; martingale posteriors of Fong, Holmes and Walker (2023); the inverse Sanov theorem (Ganesh and O'Connell 1999); the sandwich covariance under misspecification (White 1982; Muller 2013).

## New results

The new statements are the Bayesian Sanov principle under misspecification, the non coverage exponents and three regimes results on moderate scales, the calibration results for tempered and variational posteriors under misspecification and dependence, the functional Bernstein and von Mises theorem at proportional dimension, and the moment gap and admissibility separation results for martingale posteriors.

- [Moment gap of martingale posteriors](../RESULTS.md#moment_gap_martingale_posterior) (Proposition 7.8, page 238; proved): A martingale of posterior means does not determine the k step run probability from the current value alone, although the whole path of values does. First stated in Polson and Zantedeschi (2026a), Martingale Posterior Predictive Coherence: Hausdorff Moment Hierarchy.
- [Predictive variance gap for martingale posteriors](../RESULTS.md#predictive_variance_gap) (Corollary 7.9, page 238; proved): Under the martingale posterior condition the current value does not determine the predictive variance or the coverage of a prediction set for the next k observations when k is at least 2. First stated in Polson and Zantedeschi (2026a), Martingale Posterior Predictive Coherence: Hausdorff Moment Hierarchy.
- [Blackwell admissible, anytime valid and conformal rules are non nested](../RESULTS.md#admissibility_classes_non_nested) (Proposition 7.11, page 242; proved): The classes of Blackwell admissible, anytime valid admissible and conformal valid rules are pairwise non nested, and the martingale condition is necessary for the first but does not characterize any of them. First stated in Polson and Zantedeschi (2026b), Bayes with No Shame: Admissibility Geometries of Predictive Inference.
- [Bayesian Sanov principle under misspecification](../RESULTS.md#bayesian_sanov) (Proposition 7.20, page 255; proved): On a finite alphabet the posterior obeys a large deviation principle at speed n whose rate is the excess reverse relative entropy over the reverse information projection of the truth; the well specified case is the inverse Sanov theorem of Ganesh and O'Connell (1999). First stated in Polson and Zantedeschi (2026j), Bayesian Asymptotics: Merging, Martingales, and Moderate Deviations, SSRN 7013738.
- [Non coverage exponents of credible sets on moderate scales](../RESULTS.md#non_coverage_exponents_of_credible_sets) (Corollary 7.25, page 260; proved): On moderate scales credible balls and confidence balls have the same Gaussian tail exponent, which sharpens the Bernstein and von Mises equivalence to an exponential rate. First stated in Polson and Zantedeschi (2026j), Bayesian Asymptotics: Merging, Martingales, and Moderate Deviations, SSRN 7013738.
- [Three regimes of the posterior governed by one quadratic form](../RESULTS.md#three_regimes_one_rate_function) (Proposition 7.26, page 261; proved): Combining the Bernstein and von Mises theorem, the moderate deviation theorem of Eichelsbacher and Ganesh and the Bayesian Sanov principle, the central limit, moderate deviation and large deviation behavior of the posterior are one rate function seen at three magnifications, with the Fisher information as its Hessian. First stated in Polson and Zantedeschi (2026j), Bayesian Asymptotics: Merging, Martingales, and Moderate Deviations, SSRN 7013738.
- [Functional Bernstein and von Mises and bulk non contraction at proportional rate](../RESULTS.md#functional_bvm_proportional_asymptotics) (Theorem 7.30, page 271; proved): In Gaussian linear regression with dimension proportional to sample size, one dimensional functionals of the posterior are Gaussian with inflated variance and satisfy a deformed moderate deviation principle while the full posterior does not contract. First stated in Polson and Zantedeschi (2026j), Bayesian Asymptotics: Merging, Martingales, and Moderate Deviations, SSRN 7013738.
- [Moderate scale calibration of tempered posteriors under misspecification](../RESULTS.md#moderate_scale_calibration_misspecification) (Proposition 7.31, page 272; proved): A scalar learning rate calibrates credible balls at moderate scale only when the model curvature and score covariance are proportional, and otherwise a matrix temperature is needed. First stated in Polson and Zantedeschi (2026j), Bayesian Asymptotics: Merging, Martingales, and Moderate Deviations, SSRN 7013738.
- [Best scalar temperature for a misspecified posterior](../RESULTS.md#best_scalar_temperature) (Corollary 7.32, page 273; proved): When no scalar learning rate calibrates, the rate that matches the expected credible radius to nominal is the dimension divided by the trace of the product of inverse curvature and score covariance. First stated in Polson and Zantedeschi (2026j), Bayesian Asymptotics: Merging, Martingales, and Moderate Deviations, SSRN 7013738.
- [Moderate deviation form of mean field variational undercoverage](../RESULTS.md#mean_field_variational_anti_conservatism) (Proposition 7.33, page 274; proved): In the Gaussian limit the product form variational approximation, whose marginal variances are classically too small, has a larger moderate deviation rate than the posterior, so its credible intervals are too narrow by a factor given by the coordinate curvature product. First stated in Polson and Zantedeschi (2026j), Bayesian Asymptotics: Merging, Martingales, and Moderate Deviations, SSRN 7013738.
- [Calibration under dependent data and the long run variance](../RESULTS.md#dependent_data_calibration) (Proposition 7.34, page 275; conditional on the hypothesis that the posterior precision is asymptotically n V under stationary alpha mixing data and a possibly misspecified model, which the book assumes and does not derive): For mixing data the Bernstein and von Mises limit has the sandwich form with the long run score variance, and the calibrating temperature is the curvature over that variance in the scalar case. First stated in Polson and Zantedeschi (2026j), Bayesian Asymptotics: Merging, Martingales, and Moderate Deviations, SSRN 7013738.
