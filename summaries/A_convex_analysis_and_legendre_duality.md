This appendix is background. It collects the convex analysis used throughout the book: the Legendre Fenchel transform (convex conjugate), Fenchel's equality and the biconjugate theorem, the log partition function of an exponential family as a cumulant generating function, and Bregman divergences. It explains why the Legendre duality between natural and mean parameters is the same duality that turns a large deviation rate function into a cumulant generating function, so that, when the base measure h is a probability density, Cramer's rate function for the mean of the sufficient statistic of i.i.d. draws from h is the conjugate of the log partition function. Bregman divergences unify squared error and Kullback Leibler divergence, to be induced by every strictly proper scoring rule through the Savage representation, and to satisfy a generalized Pythagorean theorem that is the convex analytic form of the I projection identity. The appendix states these facts without proof under standard regularity assumptions; it is a reference for the free energy and rate function correspondence used from the information chapter onward.

## From the literature

Fenchel's equality and the biconjugate theorem for closed convex functions. Cramer's theorem for the rate function of sample means. Bregman divergences (Bregman 1967) and the Savage representation of proper scoring rules. The I projection identity (Csiszar 1975) and its generalized Pythagorean form for Bregman projections.
