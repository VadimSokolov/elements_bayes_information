This appendix is background. It reviews exchangeability and de Finetti's theorem, the starting point of the predictive approach. A sequence is exchangeable if its law is invariant under finite permutations, a weaker condition than independence and the symmetry that Bayesian modeling assumes. De Finetti's theorem represents an infinite exchangeable sequence of binary variables as a mixture of i.i.d. Bernoulli laws, with the mixing measure the law of the almost sure limit of the empirical frequency; the Hewitt Savage extension covers Polish spaces through a directing random measure. Finite exchangeable sequences need not be mixtures of i.i.d. laws (sampling without replacement), but a length k prefix of an n exchangeable sequence is within total variation of order k squared over n of one. The Hewitt Savage 0 1 law, that exchangeable events have probability 0 or 1 under an i.i.d. law, underlies posterior consistency and merging of opinions.

## From the literature

de Finetti's representation theorem (de Finetti 1937). The Hewitt Savage extension to general spaces and the Hewitt Savage 0 1 law (Hewitt and Savage 1955). Approximation of finite exchangeable sequences by mixtures of i.i.d. laws, with error of order k squared over n (Diaconis and Freedman 1980).
