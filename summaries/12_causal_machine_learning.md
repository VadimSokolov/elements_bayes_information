How can causal effects and decisions be estimated reliably when flexible machine learning supplies the nuisance fits, labels are scarce, and likelihoods are intractable? Double machine learning (DML) uses Neyman orthogonal scores and cross fitting to estimate a structural effect at the root n rate despite slow nuisance learners. Prediction powered inference (PPI) combines a small labeled sample with a large unlabeled pool through a generalized control variate (GCV) estimator, which unifies Bayes linear adjusted expectation, cross prediction powered inference and inverse probability weighted PPI, and links to the Berliner and Hill survival estimator and martingale posteriors. Generative Bayesian computation (GBC) learns a quantile neural network transport map from prior simulations to the posterior for density free inference on treatment effects and utility based policies; several of its proofs are sketches resting on assumed Bernstein and von Mises expansions. A closing discussion builds the method of types e value from Sanov's theorem.

## From the literature

- Double machine learning with Neyman orthogonal scores and cross fitting (Chernozhukov et al. 2018; Neyman 1959).
- Cross prediction powered inference (Zrnic and Candes 2024); prediction powered inference (Angelopoulos et al. 2023).
- No semi supervised gain for a correctly specified linear model (Chakrabortty and Cai 2018), and the augmented inverse probability weighted estimator with its double robustness (Robins, Rotnitzky and Zhao 1994).
- The Horvitz and Thompson estimator (1952), Kaplan and Meier estimation (1958), inverse probability of censoring weighting (Robins and Rotnitzky 1992).
- Hill's A(n) rule (Hill 1968, 1988), the Berliner and Hill survival estimator (1988), conditionally identically distributed sequences (Berti, Pratelli and Rigo 2004) and martingale posteriors (Fong, Holmes and Walker 2023).
- Brenier's optimal transport map (Brenier 1991), implicit quantile networks (Dabney et al. 2018), and the efficiency bound for the average treatment effect (Chamberlain 1987; Hahn 1998; Hirano, Imbens and Ridder 2003).

## New results

The control variate unification, the gain on residual reading and the Berliner and Hill limit come from the PPI paper, the quantile network consistency, efficiency and policy contraction results from the GBC paper, and the method of types e value from the paper on e values and Sanov's theorem.

- [Generalized control variate estimator, optimal shrinkage and variance](../RESULTS.md#generalized_control_variate_unification) (Theorem 12.6, page 394; proved): The estimators of Bayes linear, cross prediction powered inference and inverse probability weighted prediction powered inference are all a baseline plus a shrunken labeled data rectifier, and the best shrinkage is a covariance over variance ratio. First stated in Polson, Sokolov and Zantedeschi (2026f), Linear Bayes Methods for Prediction-Powered Inference, SSRN 6744239.
- [Bayes linear and cross prediction powered inference as gain on residual updates](../RESULTS.md#bl_and_cross_ppi_gain_on_residual) (Theorem 12.10, page 397; proved): Bayes linear and cross prediction powered inference are both a starting point plus a gain times a labeled residual, and they differ only in the gain, which for Cross PPI ignores the labeled data precision. First stated in Polson, Sokolov and Zantedeschi (2026f), Linear Bayes Methods for Prediction-Powered Inference, SSRN 6744239.
- [Uniform A_(n) predictive rule is conditionally identically distributed with martingale posterior CDF](../RESULTS.md#uniform_a_n_rule_is_cid_martingale_posterior_cdf) (Theorem 12.17, page 401; proved): Hill's uniform predictive rule is a conditionally identically distributed sequence, and the martingale posterior mean of the distribution function is the empirical count over n plus one. First stated in Polson, Sokolov and Zantedeschi (2026f), Linear Bayes Methods for Prediction-Powered Inference, SSRN 6744239.
- [Berliner and Hill survival estimator as a limit of Bayes linear adjusted expectations](../RESULTS.md#berliner_hill_limit_of_bayes_linear) (Theorem 12.18, page 402; proved): The Berliner and Hill survival estimator is the Kaplan and Meier product with one extra virtual observation per risk set, a limit of Bayes linear estimates and, without censoring, a martingale posterior mean. First stated in Polson, Sokolov and Zantedeschi (2026f), Linear Bayes Methods for Prediction-Powered Inference, SSRN 6744239.
- [Wasserstein consistency of the quantile neural network posterior](../RESULTS.md#qnn_posterior_wasserstein_consistency) (Theorem 12.30, page 413; proved): For a scalar parameter, the posterior obtained by pushing uniform draws through a trained quantile network is within its integrated quantile error plus the sufficiency gap of the summary statistic of the exact posterior in Wasserstein distance, so it converges when both errors vanish. First stated in Polson, Sokolov and Zantedeschi (2026), Generative Bayesian Computation: A Measure-Theoretic Framework for Utility-Based Causal Inference, working paper.
- [Efficiency of the GBC average treatment effect estimator in a parametric structural causal model](../RESULTS.md#gbc_ate_parametric_efficiency) (Theorem 12.35, page 415; proved): In a correctly specified parametric structural causal model, the average of the treatment effect over GBC posterior draws is asymptotically normal with the parametric information bound as variance, which never exceeds the nonparametric efficiency bound and equals it only when the efficient influence function lies in the span of the model scores. First stated in Polson, Sokolov and Zantedeschi (2026), Generative Bayesian Computation: A Measure-Theoretic Framework for Utility-Based Causal Inference, working paper.
- [Posterior contraction of the GBC maximum expected utility policy](../RESULTS.md#gbc_meu_policy_posterior_contraction) (Theorem 12.41, page 421; proved): Under a Lipschitz utility, posterior contraction at a rate and a quadratic margin condition, the policy that maximizes posterior expected utility converges in mean square to the oracle policy at a rate proportional to the contraction rate, so at rate N to the minus one quarter in parametric models. First stated in Polson, Sokolov and Zantedeschi (2026), Generative Bayesian Computation: A Measure-Theoretic Framework for Utility-Based Causal Inference, working paper.
- [Bayes regret of the GBC maximum expected utility policy](../RESULTS.md#gbc_meu_bayes_regret) (Corollary 12.42, page 422; proved): Without any margin condition, the expected utility lost by the policy that maximizes posterior expected utility, relative to the oracle policy, is of the order of the posterior contraction rate, so of order one over root N in parametric models. First stated in Polson, Sokolov and Zantedeschi (2026), Generative Bayesian Computation: A Measure-Theoretic Framework for Utility-Based Causal Inference, working paper.
- [Method of types e value with Kullback Leibler growth rate](../RESULTS.md#method_of_types_e_value) (Theorem 12.53, page 430; proved): Dividing the maximum likelihood ratio against a simple null on a finite alphabet by a polynomial bound on the number of types gives a valid e value, whose logarithm grows at the Kullback Leibler divergence of the true law from the null. First stated in Polson, Sokolov and Zantedeschi (2026), E-Values and Sanov's Theorem, working paper.
