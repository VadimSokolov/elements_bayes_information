What does conditioning on information do to a predictive distribution, and how are the resulting projections computed? This chapter treats moment conditioning as projection onto an affine family, with relative entropy as squared distance and a Pythagorean theorem. Conditioning an independent sample from a reference law on its empirical moments gives an exact mixture over empirical types. On the constraint chart the mixing law is asymptotically Gaussian with precision given by a reduced Hessian, and a fixed block of draws collapses to independent draws from the I projection (the exponential tilt), with explicit total variation bounds for exactly feasible and generic windowed constraints. Parameterizing the projected family gives a predictive product criterion that is locally a generalized method of moments objective, related to exponentially tilted empirical likelihood. The second half treats maximum entropy algorithms as one master routine, alternating minimization, with iterative proportional fitting and iterative scaling, Dykstra and Bregman projection, Blahut and Arimoto, and majorization as its faces, and shows that the Gibbs rule is an entropic proximal operator. It also gives a counterexample showing that the mutual information Hessian in a channel parameter need not be positive semidefinite.

## From the literature

The I projection theorem, Pythagorean identity and Gibbs form of the minimizer (Csiszar 1975). Sanov's theorem and the conditional limit theorem (Sanov 1957; van Campenhout and Cover 1981; Csiszar 1984). Alternating minimization, the five point property and convergence (Csiszar and Tusnady 1984). Cyclic I projection onto affine sets and generalized iterative scaling (Csiszar 1975; Darroch and Ratcliff 1972). Dykstra's algorithm (Dykstra 1985; Censor and Reich 1998). Blahut and Arimoto algorithms (Blahut 1972; Arimoto 1972). Empirical likelihood, exponentially tilted empirical likelihood and efficient generalized method of moments (Owen 1988, 2001; Qin and Lawless 1994; Schennach 2005).

## New results

The chapter's new results are the collapse, localization and moment criterion theorems of the moment conditioning section (Polson and Zantedeschi 2025b), which depend on supplementary proofs, and the counterexample on the mutual information Hessian (2026d).

- [Gaussian localization on the constraint chart](../RESULTS.md#gaussian_localization_constraint_chart) (Theorem 5.8, page 155; conditional on the bad type suppression and good type Gaussian control lemmas and the fixed chart Taylor, Stirling and coarea remainder constants, which the book states with outline proofs only and defers to the Supplementary Material of the source paper): The conditioned predictive law of a block is a Gaussian mixture of product laws centered at the I projection, with precision given by the reduced Hessian. - [Exchangeable block collapse under moment conditioning](../RESULTS.md#exchangeable_block_collapse) (Theorem 5.10, page 156; conditional on the Gaussian localization theorem on the constraint chart, whose proof the book only outlines, deferring the bad type suppression and good type Gaussian control lemmas and the fixed chart remainder constants to the Supplementary Material of the source paper; the step from localization to the collapse bound is proved in the book): Conditioning an independent sample on its empirical moments makes any fixed block of draws asymptotically independent from the I projection, with an explicit total variation bound in the block size and the reduced Hessian. First stated in Polson and Zantedeschi (2025b), Bayesian Prediction under Moment Conditioning.
- [Window robust collapse without lattice regularity](../RESULTS.md#window_robust_collapse) (Theorem 5.11, page 156; conditional on the conditional concentration of the empirical cell frequencies around the slab minimizer at rate the square root of log n over n, and the distance of order the tolerance between the slab minimizer and the I projection, both cited in the book's outline proof from the Supplementary Material of the source paper): For generic real valued moments, where exact feasibility fails, conditioning on a shrinking tolerance window still makes a fixed block asymptotically independent from the I projection, with a coarser rate. First stated in Polson and Zantedeschi (2025b), Bayesian Prediction under Moment Conditioning.
- [Lifted collapse to the ambient predictive law under refinement](../RESULTS.md#lifted_diagonal_collapse) (Corollary 5.15, page 159; conditional on the projection approximation lemma (Lemma S3 of the Supplementary Material of the source paper), which bounds the total variation between the lifted chart projection and the ambient I projection by the cell oscillation, and the window robust collapse bound): Letting the partition refine slowly with the sample size, the conditioned block law lifted back to the sample space converges to independent draws from the ambient I projection. First stated in Polson and Zantedeschi (2025b), Bayesian Prediction under Moment Conditioning.
- [Projection induced moment criterion is a generalized method of moments](../RESULTS.md#projection_induced_moment_criterion) (Proposition 5.20, page 163; conditional on the dual expansion proof in the Supplementary Material of the source paper, which the book does not reproduce): The negative log of the induced conditional likelihood is locally a quadratic form in the moment residuals weighted by the inverse cell moment covariance, as in efficient generalized method of moments. First stated in Polson and Zantedeschi (2025b), Bayesian Prediction under Moment Conditioning.
- [Mutual information Hessian in the channel parameter need not be positive semidefinite](../RESULTS.md#mutual_information_hessian_not_psd) (Proposition 5.33, page 188; counterexample, proved): The second derivative of mutual information with respect to a softmax channel parameter can be negative, so a published identification diagnostic that treats it as positive semidefinite is not valid. First stated in Polson and Zantedeschi (2026d), Maximum Entropy Algorithms for Predictive Rules: Blahut-Arimoto, Conditional Limits, and Iterative Scaling, working paper.
