How do regularization, nonparametric models and averaging fit the predictive view of Bayes? The chapter treats every penalty as a prior and every prior as a predictive commitment. It covers Bayesian regularization, sparse recovery and empirical Bayes (the horseshoe prior, Tweedie's formula, the lasso and global local shrinkage), discrete choice and the multinomial probit with latent utility, rare event simulation with split sampling and slice sampling, Bayesian model averaging and predictive distributions, Gaussian processes, variational inference, Bayesian additive regression trees, hierarchical models and partial pooling, Dirichlet process mixtures and clustering, and model criticism with posterior predictive checks. The common themes are the marginal likelihood as Occam's razor, shrinkage geometry with a spike for null coordinates and a tail for signals, and averaging rather than selection as the logarithmic loss optimal way to carry model uncertainty. The chapter is a survey; it catalogues no new theorem.
